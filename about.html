Six Decades of Stillness

How a private family estate in the amber hills became one of the continent's most cherished private sanctuaries.

The Origin

Built on the Measure of Silence

In the winter of 1962, when the first foundation stones of The Grand Velora were laid, Jaipur was a city enclosed by fragrance and sandstone walls. The estate was envisioned not as a hotel, but as a refuge — a place where scholars, artists, and statesmen gathered to step outside the clock.

Master stonemasons chiseled each arch by hand from local pink and amber sandstone, orienting every chamber to catch morning breezes and evening shade. No trees were felled; the pavilions were shaped around existing ancient neem and banyan roots.

"We do not inherit a building. We inherit the light that passes through its courtyards."

Milestones

A Living Heritage

The evolution of hospitality practiced as a deliberate craft.

1962

The Foundation Stone

Commissioned as a secluded winter palace for royal guests and international diplomats. The iconic octagonal courtyard and central water court were completed under royal patronage.

1989

The Great Restoration

Transitioned into an exclusive 30-key luxury guest sanctuary. Artisans spent two years restoring hand-painted ceiling frescoes and installing bespoke brass hardware.

2008

The Subterranean Baths

Excavation and unveiling of The Azure Cistern and the Soma Ayurvedic apothecary, introducing world-renowned holistic restorative therapies to the estate.

Today

Sixty-Two Sanctuaries

Continuously recognized as one of the world's finest boutique heritage properties, remaining devoted to absolute privacy, mindful service, and quiet luxury.
